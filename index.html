Abstract

The aviation industry contributes significantly to global greenhouse gas emissions, largely due to the combustion of jet fuel. Electric propulsion offers significant potential to reduce this environmental impact by lowering carbon emissions, decreasing noise pollution, and improving energy efficiency. This paper explores these benefits, ongoing electric aircraft projects, and challenges to widespread adoption.

Introduction

Aviation accounts for around 2–3% of global carbon dioxide emissions (IATA, 2023). Technological advancements, particularly in electric propulsion, could greatly reduce these impacts. This paper discusses potential environmental gains, including lower emissions, less noise, and better efficiency.

The Environmental Impact of Aviation

The sector emits large amounts of CO₂, nitrogen oxides, and particulates. In 2019, aviation emitted ~915 million metric tonnes of CO₂ (ATAG, 2021). Non-CO₂ effects, such as contrails, can also have significant warming potential (ICAO, 2020).

Electric Engines and Their Potential Environmental Benefits

Electric propulsion, powered by renewable energy, can virtually eliminate in-flight carbon emissions. Studies show potential reductions in CO₂ by 60–80% depending on the energy source (NREL, 2022).

Energy Efficiency and Reduced Fuel Consumption

Electric motors can convert over 90% of electrical energy into thrust, compared to ~40% for combustion engines (Butler, 2020). This improves efficiency and reduces overall energy demand.

Noise Pollution Reduction

Electric engines produce significantly less noise, particularly during takeoff and landing. NASA research indicates potential reductions of up to 50% (NASA, 2019), improving quality of life near airports.

Current Electric Aircraft Projects

Examples include Eviation’s “Alice” electric commuter aircraft and Airbus’s hybrid-electric “E-Fan X” project, with a goal of a zero-emission commercial aircraft by 2035.

Challenges and Limitations

Battery energy density and weight currently limit range. Infrastructure, such as charging facilities, also needs significant investment.

Conclusion

Electric propulsion offers real environmental benefits but requires overcoming technical and infrastructure challenges for mainstream adoption.
